# Privacy

Reversal collects no data. The command line, the web UI, the Mac app and the iPhone and iPad app
all work on your own device: they have no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no tracking, and
they don't connect to the internet. Nothing you open in Reversal is sent anywhere.

What Reversal keeps, all on your device:

- **DNGs and settings** go into a `positives` folder next to the scans: the converted DNGs, and
  a settings file for each frame you've adjusted. Your scans themselves are never changed.
- **Copied scans:** on iPhone and iPad, scans you copy in from Files, from a card or from your
  photo library, and raw files another app opens in Reversal, become a roll in the app's own
  folder. A roll stays until you delete it in Reversal, or delete the app.
- **The last folder** you opened, so it can open again next time.

On iPhone and iPad, raw photos reach the app through the system's photo picker, which hands over
only what you chose. Reversal asks to add to your photo library only to save DNGs there, when
you choose to. DNGs leave the app only where you send them, through the system's share sheet.

If you test Reversal through TestFlight, Apple shares some data with the developer: your name and
email address if you were invited by email, when you install and use test builds, crash reports,
and the feedback and screenshots you choose to send. Apple's
[TestFlight privacy terms](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/test-flight/) cover these.

Questions about privacy go to [GitHub issues](https://github.com/janwiktor/reversal/issues).
